# (Tentative) The Tier Rules You Should Know Before Your First Dispute

Most Pros learn how the tier system works the hard way — after something has already happened. This post is for you before that moment.

---

## What your tier score is based on

Your tier score reflects your reliability and quality on the platform. The main factors:

- **Completion rate** — the % of accepted jobs you complete
- **Rating** — average customer rating across completed jobs
- **Job volume** — consistency of work over time

These three things, tracked together, determine whether you're moving toward Blue, staying where you are, or sliding back.

---

## What counts against you (and what doesn't)

**Does count:**
- Dropping a job you've already accepted (especially close to the service date)
- A string of low customer ratings
- Incomplete jobs with no documented reason

**Does not count:**
- A customer cancelling or rescheduling on their end
- A properly documented weather or equipment reschedule
- A reported door refusal (when handled through support, not a self-drop)

The distinction that trips most Pros up: **it's not just what happened, it's how it was recorded.** A reschedule through the app is different from a drop. A reported door refusal is different from a self-initiated job drop.

---

## The rule most Pros don't know until too late

If something goes wrong on a job — the customer refuses you, conditions are unsafe, the property isn't what was described — **report it before you take any other action.** Don't drop the job first and explain later. Contact support first, document what happened, and let them handle the job status.

This sequence protects you. The reverse sequence usually doesn't.

---

## Where to find your current tier standing

Your tier status is visible in the app under your profile. It shows your current tier (Intro, Blue, Gold, Platinum) and your overall rating. For more detail on what's moving your score, check the monthly Q&A thread in the community — that's where the team answers those questions directly.

---

## One more thing: ratings work both ways

A single low rating won't tank your score if your overall pattern is strong. What does matter is a pattern — three or four low ratings in a row without a recovery. If you get a rating you think was unfair, you can flag it for review. Not every dispute goes in your favor, but the option exists.

---

*Something happened and you're not sure if it's going to affect your score? Post in the Q&A thread or reach out to support — better to ask before it shows up.*
