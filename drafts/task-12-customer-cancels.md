**When the Customer Cancels on You**

A customer reaching out to cancel isn't a crisis — but how you handle it matters for your metrics, your trip fee eligibility, and whether the cancellation processes correctly. Here's what to do.

---

**First: there's no "cancel for customer" button in the app**

The app doesn't give you a way to directly cancel a service on a customer's behalf. What you can do is submit a pause/cancel request — which tells LawnStarter the customer wants to stop, and they process it from there.

This is important because you should not just drop the customer yourself. Dropping the property is a Pro-initiated action that counts against your cancellation rate. Submitting a pause/cancel request on the customer's behalf does not.

---

**What to do — step by step**

1. **Screenshot or document the customer's message** — if they told you through the app, the message is already logged. If they called or texted outside the app, take a screenshot.
2. **Open the app → Today's Jobs → select the customer**
3. **Tap Other → Customer Requested Pause or Cancel**
4. **Answer what the customer requested** — "Pause until spring" or "Cancel entirely." Add any context in the notes field.
5. **Tap Submit** — this sends the customer a confirmation request. It does NOT automatically cancel or remove the job from your schedule. LawnStarter processes it once the customer confirms.
6. **Don't complete the job** — if the customer has explicitly said they want to cancel, do not mow.
7. **Contact support if you're unsure** — if the customer is unclear about whether they want to pause one service or cancel entirely, report it and let support sort it out.

---

**How this affects your metrics**

Two different metrics, two different rules:

**Cancellation rate:** Customer-initiated cancellations do NOT count here. Only cancellations you caused count toward this number. You're protected.

**Completion rate:** This is the one to know. *All* unfinished jobs — whether the customer cancelled, the weather was bad, or anything else — count against completion rate. The threshold is built with this in mind (Gold only requires 55%), but the hit is real and it adds up.

The takeaway: a customer cancellation doesn't hurt your cancellation rate, but it does slightly reduce your completion rate. That's why the recommended action is to submit the pause/cancel request rather than quietly absorbing the skip.

---

**Trip fees — one thing to know if you're already at the property**

If the customer gave you advance notice before you drove out, you are **not** eligible for a trip fee. Trip fees apply when you show up with no warning and can't complete the service.

If you're already at the property when the customer tells you to leave: **skip the job first before submitting the pause/cancel request** — this secures your trip fee eligibility. The path is: Today's Jobs → Customer → Other → "Customer told me to leave" → "To cancel or pause future services." Your GPS must be pinged on arrival for the trip fee to apply.

---

**Quick reference**

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Customer messages before service day | Document → submit pause/cancel request via app |
| Customer messages day-of, you haven't left yet | Document → submit pause/cancel request via app → no trip fee |
| Customer calls or texts you directly (outside app) | Screenshot → submit pause/cancel request → support if needed |
| Customer says "just pause for now" | Submit pause request — same path as cancellation |
| Tempted to just drop the customer yourself | Don't — that hits your cancellation rate. Use pause/cancel path |
