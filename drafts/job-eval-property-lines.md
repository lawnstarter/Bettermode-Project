# (Tentative) Whose Yard Is It? How to Handle Property Line Questions

You're at the property. You're not sure where the customer's yard ends and the neighbor's begins. Or you've finished the job and the neighbor is telling you that you mowed their lawn.

This happens more than you'd think. Here's how to handle it — before, during, and after.

---

## Before you start: establish the boundary

If you're at a new property and the yard boundary isn't clear:

- **Check the listing address against what's in front of you.** Does the property match in size and layout?
- **Look for natural boundary markers** — fences, driveways, landscaping edges, curbs. These aren't always property lines, but they're a starting point.
- **If in doubt, take a before-photo of the full yard** from the street. This documents what you saw before you touched anything.

Do not assume the customer's description is complete. "Just mow the front and back" leaves a lot undefined on a corner lot or a property with shared green space.

---

## If the boundary is genuinely unclear before you start

Contact the customer. A short message — "Just confirming: does your yard include the area to the left of the driveway, or does that belong to the neighbor?" — takes 30 seconds and prevents disputes that take 30 minutes to resolve.

Don't start until you have an answer on anything genuinely ambiguous.

---

## If a neighbor approaches you during the job

Stay calm. Don't argue about the boundary on the spot.

- Stop work on the disputed area
- Explain that you're completing the job as listed and will check with the customer
- Document the interaction briefly in your notes
- Contact the customer before continuing or finishing

Do not let the neighbor's complaint push you into leaving a job incomplete. Complete what you can confirm is correct, document everything, and follow up through proper channels.

---

## After the job: if a dispute comes up

If a customer or neighbor files a complaint about mowing the wrong yard:

- Your before-photos are your best protection
- Your arrival timestamp shows when you were there
- Any customer communication you had about boundaries is evidence

This is another reason why the "photo before you start" habit matters for every new property — not just the ones that look complicated.

---

## Corner lots are a specific case

Corner lots are frequently listed with square footage that doesn't account for the full area. The street-facing strips on two sides can push the actual mowing area significantly above the listed size. If you're claiming a corner lot job, check satellite view before accepting to confirm what you're looking at.
