# Before You Accept — How to Read a Job Listing and Spot Red Flags

The moment a job hits your feed, the clock starts. It's easy to claim fast and figure out the details later. But what you learn after you accept is the most expensive kind of education.

Here's how to slow down the first five seconds and actually evaluate what you're committing to.

---

## What the listing tells you (and what it doesn't)

Every job listing shows you the basics: location, lot size, service type, and payout. Those numbers are a starting point, not a guarantee.

**Lot size is an estimate.** Square footage on the listing comes from county records or customer input — neither is verified. A 5,000 sq ft listing might be 6,500 on the ground. Know that going in.

**"Customer-stated" conditions are unverified.** When a customer describes their lawn as "average height" or says there's no debris, that's self-reported. Treat it as a guess, not a fact.

**Terrain and slope aren't on the listing.** The app won't tell you if the yard is on a hillside, has a narrow gate, or requires maneuvering a large trailer. That's on you to evaluate before you go.

---

## The two questions to ask before you claim

**1. Does the payout work for the actual job — not the listed job?**
Before you accept, look at the address on a map. Pull up satellite view. Check the terrain, the lot shape, nearby obstacles. If you're on a ride-on and the listing shows a property that looks like it needs a walk-behind, the math changes.

**2. Does this job fit in my route?**
A job that's 20 minutes off your route isn't the same as one that's already on the way. Factor gas and drive time into what you're actually making.

---

## Red flags worth slowing down for

- Lot size that seems significantly smaller than nearby properties (may be miscalculated)
- First-time customer with no history — no prior ratings or reviews to reference
- "Last cleaned: 6+ months ago" — this is often an indicator of more work than the listing reflects
- No customer photo of the property — you're working with the listing description only
- Long grass noted by the customer — confirm whether the long grass upcharge is reflected in the payout before accepting

---

## The wider claim window is there for a reason

You have more time than you feel like you do. The claim window gives you a chance to evaluate before committing. Use it. Reflex-claiming and then discovering the scope on arrival is how Pros end up absorbing costs they shouldn't have to absorb.

---

## What top Pros do

The most experienced Pros on this platform treat every new property like an unknown. They assume the listing is approximate, they check the satellite view, and they price for what they actually expect to do — not what the listing describes.

That habit is learnable. It just takes practice front-loading the evaluation instead of doing it at the property.
